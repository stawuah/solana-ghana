Executive Summary
Ghana has never had an organized Solana community — no chapter, no ambassador network, no structured on-ramp. That's the gap we're closing.
We're not proposing an event. We're proposing infrastructure: a talent pipeline running through universities and senior high schools, a homegrown Rust-to-Solana curriculum already built and live on GitHub, a security and developer-assurance culture from day one, and a repeatable build-event engine that turns attendees into contributors instead of just audiences. We're doing this self-funded, starting with $200 of our own money, because we believe the strongest case for support is proof, not a pitch.
What follows is our plan to build that proof — and the vision for what Solana Ghana becomes once it does.
1. Why This Matters
There is currently no organized Solana community in Ghana. Ghana has a young, mobile-first technical population, active universities, and growing developer interest in Web3 — but no dedicated on-ramp connecting local builders to the Solana ecosystem.
We're starting that on-ramp now, as an independent community, with the goal of building the numbers and track record needed to eventually be recognized as an official Superteam Ghana chapter.
2. Near-Term Vision
Solana Ghana Community: the gateway through which Ghanaian builders learn, build, and reach the Solana ecosystem.
Long-term, we see this growing into a hub connecting Ghanaian (and eventually West African) developers to Solana through structured learning, real build events, and pathways into global opportunities like Colosseum hackathons and Solana Foundation grants. Short-term, we're deliberately starting small, unfunded, and focused on proof over promises.
3. Long-Term Vision — What We're Building Toward
We're starting lean and unfunded, but the direction we want to grow into is deliberate. Over time, we want Solana Ghana to stand out not by outspending other chapters, but by out-building them on structure, reach, and local relevance — becoming the region's reference model for what a self-starting Superteam community can look like before it ever receives outside funding.
University Ambassador Network

Campus Ambassadors — a named student lead at each partner institution (starting with Ghana Telecom University), equipped with our starter curriculum, presentation materials, a Solana Ghana Discord role, and a direct line to the core team

4. FLAGSHIP EXPERIMENT: Ghana Stablecoin Payments & Commerce Sandbox
This is our flagship long-term experiment, and gives the proposal a strong Ghanaian use-case focus. It remains a controlled prototype and research environment until appropriate legal and compliance review is completed — no real-money pilots, on/off-ramp, or custody under this plan. Those would require separate legal, compliance and commercial partnerships.
The emphasis is on settlement infrastructure and business operations, not token speculation: testnet merchant payments, QR checkout, payment links, receipts, refunds, and reconciliation without initially holding customer funds or launching a token.

This is a supporting function across all tracks, not a standalone track — and, like the Security Lab, we see it growing into its own sub-community within Solana Ghana over time.
It should produce:
Quarterly Ghana ecosystem reports
Participant and developer-needs surveys
Stablecoin and infrastructure research
Regulatory roundtables with qualified professionals

We are prioritizing two starting audiences:
University students — beginning outreach with Ghana Telecom University, targeting CS/IT departments and student tech clubs, and installing our first Campus Ambassador

12. What Makes This Different
Most new Solana communities run generic meetups and hope for organic growth. We're starting with things most chapters build only after funding arrives:
A real curriculum, not just links to docs — a test-driven, self-paced learning path already live on GitHub
A university ambassador network from day one — not an afterthought, but the core structure for how we scale beyond one city
A security and developer-assurance culture built in from the start — not bolted on after an incident
Original research — our Speed Test track produces public data on Solana network performance from Ghana that doesn't currently exist anywhere
This is why we believe Solana Ghana is fundable: not because we're asking early, but because we're building the receipts first.
